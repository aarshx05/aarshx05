Methodology: All three repositories (securedrop, securedrop-client, securedrop-workstation) were cloned and each finding was verified against the actual source code at the specific file and line cited. Findings were then cross-referenced against the BugCrowd scope document to identify which are explicitly excluded from the bug bounty program.

Key insight: Of the 42 verified findings, only 7 are both truly exploitable AND not covered by BugCrowd exclusions. The remainder are either low-severity hardening issues, informational items, or BugCrowd-excluded known/accepted behaviors. The 7 actionable findings are in the cryptographic layer (SD-001, SD-002), authentication logic (SD-006, SD-007), memory management (SD-009), input validation (SD-020, SD-031).

Recommendations

Priority 1 (Fix Now): SD-001 and SD-002 are in the cryptographic layer and affect the fundamental security guarantee of the system. The signature verification bypass means a MITM could substitute ciphertext without detection.

Priority 2 (Next Sprint): SD-006 (source rate limiting), SD-007 (2FA oracle), SD-009 (cache DoS). These affect authentication and availability.

Priority 3 (Planned): SD-020 (PGP validation) and SD-031 (reversed substring). These are logic bugs that could lead to unexpected behavior under specific conditions.

Accept/Defer: The remaining 35 verified findings are real but low-severity hardening issues. Many are defense-in-depth improvements (AppArmor, path handling, timing oracles) that reduce attack surface without addressing immediate exploitation vectors. Prioritize based on available engineering resources.
