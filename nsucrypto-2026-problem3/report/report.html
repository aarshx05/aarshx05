Problem 3. The BSW Conjecture

NSUCRYPTO 2026, Second round — solution

Summary of what is proved

The problem asks to prove Conjecture 1 for all qq, for an infinite class of qq, or for finitely many qq. The case q≡1(mod4)q\equiv 1 \;(\mathrm{mod}\;4) is the theorem of Blokhuis, Seress and Wilbrink (BSW). Everything below concerns the open case q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3 \;(\mathrm{mod}\;4).

  1. Conjecture 1 holds for all 61 prime powers q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3 \;(\mathrm{mod}\;4) with 31<q<70031<q<700 (computer-assisted; the algorithm is proved correct in Section 5 and was checked by a second, unrelated program). Together with BSW this gives: Conjecture 1 holds for every odd prime power 31<q<70031<q<700.

  2. Theorem A (every q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3 \;(\mathrm{mod}\;4), no lower bound). The hypotheses of Conjecture 1 say exactly that ψ\psi preserves a certain “orientation” of all triples of points that contain a pair {x,ψx}\{x,\psi x\}. The conclusion ψ(x)=n/x\psi(x)=n/x holds if and only if ψ\psi preserves the orientation of all triples, and this is the case if and only if no three pairs form a “negative triangle”, where the sign of a triangle has the closed form χ((1−λ1)(1−λ2)(1−λ3))\chi((1-\lambda_1)(1-\lambda_2)(1-\lambda_3)) in terms of the three pairwise cross-ratios. So the conjecture is equivalent to a purely combinatorial statement about triangles.

  3. Theorem B (stability, every odd qq). If ψ(x)=n/x\psi(x)=n/x holds for more than q+2q−72\tfrac{q+2\sqrt q-7}{2} elements xx, then it holds for all xx.

  4. Theorem C (spreading, every odd qq). A map ψ\psi that satisfies the hypotheses but is not of the form n/xn/x agrees with any map x↦n′/xx\mapsto n'/x on at most 7q+127\sqrt q+12 elements xx. A counterexample would have to be “spread out” with respect to every candidate n′n'.

  5. The bound q>31q>31 cannot be lowered. For q=7,11,19,23,27,31q=7,11,19,23,27,31 there are maps ψ\psi satisfying all hypotheses that are not of the form n/xn/x. Their existence was already stated by BSW (q=7,11q=7,11 go back to Korchmáros, 1981); here they are listed explicitly, and the q=7,11,31q=7,11,31 ones are explained as “antipodal maps” of the rotation groups of the cube and the icosahedron.

  6. Two natural proof strategies for the general case are shown to be impossible (Section 7): no clique-bound (Hoffman / Delsarte / Lovász theta) certificate can be tight, and the involutions attached to the counterexamples generate the whole group PSL(2,q)PSL(2,q).

The conjecture for all qq, or for an infinite class, remains open. All programs, raw outputs and the counterexamples are supplied with this report (Appendix).

1. Notation and a reformulation as a matching problem

Definition. Two disjoint pairs (we call them chords) {a,b}\{a,b\} and {c,d}\{c,d\} cross if χ((a,b;c,d))=−1.\chi((a,b;c,d))=-1.

Lemma 1.1 (dictionary). A map ψ\psi satisfies the hypotheses of Conjecture 1 if and only if Mψ={{0,∞}}∪{{x,ψx}:x∈F*}M_\psi=\big\{\{0,\infty\}\big\}\cup\big\{\{x,\psi x\}:x\in F^*\big\} is a perfect matching of PG(1,q)PG(1,q) (a partition into q+12\tfrac{q+1}{2} chords) in which every two chords cross. Proof. ψ\psi has no fixed point, because it changes the quadratic character. We call such a matching a good matching, and we call it canonical if it is the orbit set of some Möbius involution without fixed points (an elliptic involution; in suitable coordinates every such involution is z↦n/zz\mapsto n/z with nn a non-square). Lemma 1.2. For every non-square nn the map ψ(x)=n/x\psi(x)=n/x satisfies the hypotheses.

Proof. x⋅ψ(x)=nx\cdot\psi(x)=n is a non-square. For y∉{x,n/x}y\notin\{x,n/x\}, (x−y)(x−ny)(nx−y)(nx−ny)=n(x−y)2(xy−n)2x2y2,(x-y)(x-\frac ny)(\frac nx-y)(\frac nx-\frac ny)=\frac{n\,(x-y)^2\,(xy-n)^2}{x^2y^2}, whose character is χ(n)=−1\chi(n)=-1. ▫\square

Geometric meaning. Identify PG(1,q)PG(1,q) with the conic CC of the problem statement; a chord is a secant line. Two chords cross exactly when the two secants meet in an interior point. So a good matching is a set of q+12\frac{q+1}{2} secants, any two of which meet in an interior point, and “canonical” means they all pass through one interior point. Taking poles turns this into Conjecture 2.

2. Orientation of triples when q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3 \;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)

From here to the end of Section 3 assume q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4), so χ(−1)=−1\chi(-1)=-1.

For three distinct points u,v,wu,v,w define the orientation o(u,v,w)=χ([u,v][v,w][w,u])∈{±1}.o(u,v,w)=\chi([u,v]\,[v,w]\,[w,u])\in\{\pm1\}. Each point occurs twice, so oo does not depend on the representatives. For a Möbius map gg with matrix of determinant detg\det g one has [gu,gv]=detg⋅[u,v]⋅(a square)[gu,gv]=\det g\cdot[u,v]\cdot(\text{a square}), hence o(gu,gv,gw)=χ(detg)o(u,v,w).o(gu,gv,gw)=\chi(\det g)\,o(u,v,w). Theorem A. Let q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4) (any size) and let ψ\psi satisfy the hypotheses of Conjecture 1. The following are equivalent.

  1. ψ(x)=n/x\psi(x)=n/x for some non-square nn.
  2. MψM_\psi has no negative triangle: χ((1−λ1)(1−λ2)(1−λ3))=1\chi((1-\lambda_1)(1-\lambda_2)(1-\lambda_3))=1 for every three chords.

Proof. (1 ⇒ 3): z↦n/zz\mapsto n/z has matrix (0n10)\begin{pmatrix}0&n\\1&0\end{pmatrix} of determinant −n-n, a square because χ(−1)=χ(n)=−1\chi(-1)=\chi(n)=-1. So ψ∈PSL(2,q)\psi\in PSL(2,q) preserves oo.

(3 ⇔ 2): triples meeting some chord twice have E=1E=1 by Lemma 3.1; all other triples give Ê\widehat E.

(3 ⇒ 1): Let gg be any permutation of PG(1,q)PG(1,q) preserving oo. 5.2 The algorithm (verify_bsw.c)

For each non-square n≤n−1n\le n^{-1} the program lists all perfect matchings M⊇{{0,∞},{1,n}}M\supseteq\{\{0,\infty\},\{1,n\}\} in which every two chords cross and every two chords have rep≥n\mathrm{rep}\ge n, by depth-first search:

  • for every uncovered point ww it keeps the set of uncovered zz such that {w,z}\{w,z\} crosses every chosen chord (and, for the first three chords, also satisfies the class condition);
  • it branches on the uncovered point with the fewest candidates, over all its candidates;
  • a branch is abandoned only when some uncovered point has no candidate left, or when a candidate violates the class condition.

Every rule only discards matchings that violate the constraints, so the enumeration is exhaustive. Every complete matching found is re-checked against the definition by brute force and then tested for being canonical. Conjecture 1 holds for qq exactly when, for every nn, the only matching found is the canonical one (Lemmas 1.1 and 5.1).

7. What remains, and two approaches that cannot work

Theorems A–C show that a counterexample for large qq would have to contain negative triangles through every chord (Theorem A) and be spread out with respect to every elliptic involution (Theorem C). We also record two negative results, so that nobody repeats these attempts.

No clique-bound certificate. On the graph whose vertices are all chords, adjacent when they cross, a good matching is a clique of size q+12\frac{q+1}{2}. The method of BSW for q≡1(mod4)q\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4) is of Hoffman/Delsarte type, and its natural generalisation is a Lovász-theta certificate: a PGL(2,q)PGL(2,q)-invariant matrix ZZ with λmax(Z)=q+12\lambda_{\max}(Z)=\frac{q+1}{2}. Such a ZZ would force every maximum clique to satisfy strong regularity conditions. Averaging over PGL(2,q)PGL(2,q), ZZ may be assumed constant on the cross-ratio classes, and then tightness for the canonical clique is a linear system with one unknown per non-crossing class. theta_test.py computes it: for q=43,47,59,67q=43,47,59,67 the system has full column rank, so the only solution is Z=JZ=J, which is not a certificate. Hence no clique-bound proof exists for q≡3(mod4)q\equiv 3\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4). The reason is visible in Lemma 4.1: the number of canonical chords crossed by an outside chord is governed by an elliptic-curve point count and is far from constant.

No group-theoretic shortcut. Every pair of chords of a good matching determines the elliptic involution having both as orbits. One might hope that these involutions generate a small subgroup of PSL(2,q)PSL(2,q), to which Dickson’s classification could be applied. For the counterexamples with q=7,11q=7,11 they generate S4S_4 and A5A_5 and the matching is invariant; but for q=19,23,31q=19,23,31 they generate all of PSL(2,q)PSL(2,q) and the matching is not invariant (structure_probe.py).

Spectral form. With Kuv=χ([u,v][ψu,ψv])K_{uv}=\chi([u,v][\psi u,\psi v]), Theorem A says that ψ\psi is canonical iff λmax(K)=q\lambda_{\max}(K)=q. For the counterexamples λmax(K)≤11\lambda_{\max}(K)\le 11: they behave like random ±1\pm1 matrices, while the canonical one is rank-one plus identity. So a proof must establish a sharp dichotomy; there is no “almost canonical” regime to perturb from (Theorem B says the same).
